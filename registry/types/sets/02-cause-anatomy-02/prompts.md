## The rule every prompt follows

**A mark answers one question, and asks it in both panels.** Before writing a mark, the question is
named — where the weight lands, how wide the contact is, which way a joint turns — and the mark
draws that answer in each panel on the SAME named structure or path. The two answers differ in a
place, a width or a direction, never in colour alone. The one one-sided mark in the set shows what
the culprit keeps doing, the only kind that read in set 1.

| # | product | the question the mark answers | left answer | right answer | mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | memory foam seat cushion | which bone carries the weight? | the tailbone, narrow | the two sitting bones, wide | `pressure` |
| 2 | backpack with a hip belt | where does the pack's weight land? | the tops of the shoulders | the top of the pelvis | `load`, proposed |
| 3 | laptop stand | which way does the neck bend? | forward and down | upright | `contour`, anchored |
| 4 | car wash spray gun with a trigger lock | how tightly is the index finger bent? | closed, narrow | open, wide | `range`, anchored |
| 5 | cord tightening tool | what does the culprit keep doing? | the taut cord pulls against the pinch | — | `force`, one-sided |
| 6 | adjustable hand grip strengthener | which part of the fingers holds the load? | the fingertips alone | the whole fingers | `fill`, by place |

**The two marks the owner called out return redesigned, on new products.** The lifting tool's wedge
becomes `load` — where the weight goes, the question that tool actually answers. The ab roller's
floating line becomes a `contour` drawn ON the vertebrae, on a posture that changes in kind.

## RESULT — 2026-09-15, six renders, 0 pass

Six renders in `image-library-assets/feedback/` at 13:37–13:38, each mapped to its prompt by content.
Owner, same day: *"chưa có reasoning trong sử dụng màu, màu xấu dẫn đến không thể hiện được tính khoa
học của type. car wash spray gun có G3 gì đó có vẻ là sai"* — colour without a reason, bad colours,
a type that no longer reads as scientific, and a stray "G3". All true.

| # | product | verdict | what broke |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | seat cushion | partial | the pelvis upright in both panels; a second blue fill on the chair seat; a profile face |
| 2 | backpack | partial | ochre ground at 0.86 saturation; a full skeleton with a skull for a head; the red arrow double-headed; a navy product beside the blue arrow |
| 3 | laptop stand | partial | a toothed skull for a head; a second red line floating behind the neck |
| 4 | car wash spray gun | **fail** | **`G3` printed in red and blue** where the badges would sit; green grass behind both panels; red trim on the product; wedges that point at nothing |
| 5 | cord tool | **fail** | the product missing from the right panel; two red arrows; plum ground |
| 6 | grip strengthener | partial | umber ground; an orange knob in the correct panel; a badge 3.5% from the edge |

**Why the colour failed: the grounds were chosen with no reason but to differ.** Measured on the
files, set 2's grounds ran from 0.00 saturation (graphite) to 0.86 (ochre brown), with plum and
umber at 0.36–0.47. The set table justified none of them. The warm saturated fields compete with the
signal colours, so colour stopped reading as data — which is exactly what a scientific plate uses
colour for.

**Why `G3` printed.** The closing line `G3: red wrong, blue correct, nothing else.` is a library ID
the model cannot know. In the one prompt with no badge it drew the ID as a red and a blue label in
the badges' place. Set 1's other no-badge prompt did not, so this is 1 of 2 there and 1 of 11 across
both sets.

**What improved on set 1: 4 of 6 marks answered their question in both panels** — the pressure fills,
the `load` arrows, the neck line and the finger fills — against 1 of 5 in set 1. The one-question
rule works; the colour around it did not. The badge placed *above the figure* cleared G10's top
margin in 2 of 5 frames, against 1 of 4.

What moved into the type is in its 1.18 CHANGELOG. Set 3 is
`registry/types/sets/02-cause-anatomy-03/`.
