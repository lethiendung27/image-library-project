# `02-cause-anatomy` set 5 — the feature, the benefit, and the mark where they meet

Written against **`02-cause-anatomy` v1.18**. Owner, 2026-09-15, on set 4:

- *"ghế quỳ phân bổ đều trọng lượng lên 3 điểm làm thẳng lưng. ảnh hiện tại chưa đạt cho chưa thể hiện
  được benefit/feature"* — a kneeling chair spreads the weight over three points and that straightens
  the back; the render showed neither the feature nor the benefit.
- *"bút cán to chưa thể hiện rõ feature & benefit"*, *"gậy leo núi cũng chưa thể hiện rõ feature &
  benefit"* — the same for the pen and the trekking poles.
- *"gối nêm so sánh sai, không đúng anatomy"* — the wedge comparison is wrong and its anatomy wrong.

## Why the feature was missing

**The prompts never named it.** G2 keeps a product's appearance out of the prompt, and set 4 read that
as a ban on naming any part — so the kneeling chair *dropped the thighs forward* without the knee pad
ever being mentioned, and the model drew a chair with no reason to be one. G2 also allows a product's
**relation to other objects**, and *both shins rest on its knee pad* is a relation. **Every right panel
now names the part the body meets, and the mark sits at that contact.**

The wedge had a different fault: the prompt never said the reader lay on their back, and the render
drew the neck vertebrae on the throat side. **Orientation is now named.**

## Why four of the six products repeat

**The standing order is new products every set, and four of these are set 4's.** They repeat because
the owner's notes are about *these* products — the kneeling chair's three points is a correction to
that product's argument, and it cannot be applied to anything else. **Prompts 5 and 6 are new**, and
they are the test that the rule holds on products it has never seen. The shoe horn and the dog ramp,
which drew no note, do not return.

| # | product | the feature, named as a contact | the benefit | the question the mark answers | left | right |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | kneeling chair | the seat and the knee pad | the weight spreads, the spine straightens | how many points carry the weight? | one | three |
| 2 | wide-grip pen | the wide grip under the finger pads | the fingers stop clamping | how much of the finger pad presses? | a small spot | a long strip |
| 3 | trekking poles | the pole tips planted ahead | part of the weight leaves the knee | where does the weight go? | all into the knee | split into the knee and the pole |
| 4 | bed wedge pillow | the slope under the shoulders | the neck lines up with the back | which way does the neck bend? | sharply forward | in line |
| 5 | upright walker | the forearm supports at chest height | the body stands upright | where does the upper body rest? | one palm on a cane | both forearms |
| 6 | ergonomic garden trowel | the handle held in line with the forearm | the wrist stays straight | how far does the wrist bend? | sharply back | straight |

**Grounds keep set 4's reasoning**: each hue from the product's world, deep and muted — slate teal for
the office, walnut for the writing desk, heather violet for the trail at dusk, dusk blue for the
bedroom, warm taupe for a home hallway, olive for the garden bed. **No comment on colour came back on set
4, so nothing in it changes.**

### Predictions, written before the render

1. **Pass**: three blue fills against one red fill is a count, the clearest difference a mark has drawn.
2. **Partial**: the grip's width is in the reference photo; watch whether the fingers visibly relax.
3. **Partial**: two blue arrows against one red is the split; watch that the poles are planted ahead.
4. **Pass on anatomy**: on its back is now written; watch the vertebrae stay along the back of the neck.
5. **Pass**: palm on a cane against forearms on supports is a large change on a new product.
6. **Partial**: a wrist angle is small at forearm framing; the anchored wedge held once.

**Attachments: one reference photo per prompt**; where a product comes in colourways, attach one that is
not red, orange, blue or green. Ratio at the generation tool.
