# `02-cause-anatomy` set 3 — colour with a reason, written against v1.18

Owner, 2026-09-15, on set 2: *"chưa có reasoning trong sử dụng màu, màu xấu dẫn đến không thể hiện
được tính khoa học của type. car wash spray gun có G3 gì đó có vẻ là sai"* — colour was chosen
without a reason, the colours looked bad and the type stopped reading as scientific, and the car
wash render printed "G3".

## The colour system every prompt follows

1. **The ground is a clinical neutral grey**: graphite, charcoal, iron grey, gunmetal grey or smoke
   grey. Set 2 chose its grounds only to differ from each other, and they measured from 0.00 to
   0.86 saturation; the warm saturated ones — ochre brown, plum, umber — made the frame about the
   wall. **Grey keeps the signal colours the only saturated colours in the frame**, so a viewer
   reads every colour as data, and a cool neutral is the complement that makes warm ivory bone the
   brightest, warmest thing there.
2. **The ground's value is set by the product**: a pale product on the darkest grey, a dark product
   on a lighter one, so the product separates in the right panel. The value still steps lighter
   on the right (G4).
3. **Nothing stands behind the subject but the ground.** Set 2's car door close-up came back on a
   strip of green grass, which is the badge's colour, in both panels.
4. **Only the named bones are drawn; the rest is a grey outline.** Set 2 drew a full skeleton and a
   skull with teeth where three bones were listed.
5. **No `G3` in any prompt.** It is a library ID, the model cannot know what it means, and in the one
   prompt with no badge it was drawn as a red and a blue label. Each mark sentence already says
   what its colour means.
6. **No emoji badge.** A cartoon face is the least clinical form in the library; this set uses the
   glyph, the thumb, the filled warning triangle and no badge.

and nothing more: the toe spacer holds the big toe straighter while worn and does not remove a
bunion; the belt lifts the belly's weight and treats nothing.

**Attach a colourway outside red, orange, blue and green where one exists.** Set 2 put a blue

| # | product | verdict | what broke |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | gel heel cup | partial | ground pale (186–218); the whole leg skeleton drawn; a second blue dot |
| 2 | vertical mouse | partial | badges 5.2–5.5% from the top — nothing else |
| 3 | pregnancy belt | **fail** | the same spine curve in both panels; both lines float off the back |
| 4 | mattress topper | **fail** | **the ground step inverted**; the waist lies the same in both panels |
| 5 | shopping trolley | partial | shoulders level in both panels; the blue arrow runs through the trolley; ground pale (205–229) |
| 6 | toe spacer | partial | the named grey became cream engraving paper; the step went flat; extra blue lines |

**Why the colour still failed — the fix was the opposite, not a distinction.** Set 2's saturated
grounds (0.36–0.87) read as bad; set 3 answered with grey for every product (0.00–0.15), and that
reads as one colour for six different products. **The owner's two verdicts bracket the answer**: a hue
taken from each product's own world, muted rather than saturated. And set 3 dropped a word that was
doing the work: set 2's grounds named *deep* measured 43–79 of 255, while set 3's greys named without it
came back at 158–246 on four of six, and one turned into cream paper.

**What held.** Nothing stood behind the subject in 6 of 6. The positive bound — *only these bones are
drawn* — held in 4 of 6. Marks answered their question in 5 of 6, and the vertical mouse is the
clearest frame of the three sets. **What did not**: every state that changes by degree — a deeper sway,
a waist lifting, a shoulder dropping — came back unchanged, 3 of 3, leaving the marks to carry the
argument; and badges placed *above the figure* cleared G10 in 0 of 5, 2 of 10 across two sets.

What moved into the type is in its 1.18 CHANGELOG. Set 4 is
`registry/types/sets/02-cause-anatomy-04/`.
