# lede-authority

## PURPOSE
Someone who has used the thing, reporting on it. The page's credibility is carried by a
person rather than by a method — and it is carried by **CONTROL**: a composed subject, a
product in as-new condition, and a setting that is either ordered or absent. Never by a
credential printed in the frame.

**0.3 said the opposite and set 1 refuted it.** That version wrote *"the place is doing the
credentialling"* and built six prompts on lived-in realism — a worn apron, a pan just used,
a runner at the end of a run. **Four of four contextual cells came back reading as
`lede-inuse`**, and the one studio cell was the only frame in the set that read as an
authority. Grit is what a documentary photograph wants; it is not what a recommendation
wants. See FOUNDING RENDER ROUND.

## TRIGGER
use_when: >
  The page's value is first-hand experience rather than coverage or measurement — the
  reader wants to know that a human being lived with these and has something to say.
  Strongest where the category rewards practised judgement over specification, and where
  the product's own domain is a place a reader would recognise. Prefer lede-testing where
  the argument is a measurement rather than a person, and lede-inuse where the argument is
  the ordinary comfort of ownership rather than a report on it.

## BOUNDARY
**Against `lede-inuse`, and this is the line that decides the type.** That subject is
CANDID and unaware of the lens; they are living with the product. This subject is
**TESTIFYING** — addressing the lens, presenting or using the product for it. The picture is
being made *about* their opinion. Checkable at a glance, and it is the only boundary here
a reader can see.

*Until 0.5 this sentence also allowed "answering someone in the frame". The owner refused
that form on 2026-09-09; see `PARTS/address`.*

**Against `lede-testing`** — that one argues a METHOD and shows no face. No bench, no
instrument, no apparatus here. G9 still governs which is the stronger evidence, and it
prefers the physical fact; this type exists for the pages where the person IS the offer.

**Against `05-social-snapshot` and `05-social-handoff`** — those argue that a CUSTOMER
used it, in the UGC register. This is EDITORIAL: the frame is made by a photographer, not
by the subject. That distinction is not a preference, it is the G14 firewall — see
NEGATIVE.

**Against `mapping/slot-rules.md`'s `author` row** — that row refuses *"a portrait of a
named person"* for the `author` SLOT on a bylined advertorial, and its own reasoning is
that *"generating a face to sit under a real byline is a disclosure decision"*. The refusal
is about the BYLINE. A top-N lede carries no byline and names nobody, so the row does not
reach this type. See WHY THIS IS NO LONGER RESERVED.

## SKELETON
```
TYPE: lede-authority v0.11
REGISTER: editorial portrait photograph, one frame, no words in it.

[PRODUCT REFERENCE]  the attached photo is the exact reference.        -> G1
[CONDITION]          the product is clean and as-new.                  -> PARTS/condition
[SUBJECT]            one adult, dressed as the trade.                  -> PARTS/expertise
[POSE]               deliberate. Range left open to the model.         -> PARTS/pose
[ADDRESS]            to the lens. Never candid, never to a second person. -> PARTS/address
[ACT]                PRESENTING it in studio, USING it in a real place. -> PARTS/ground
[GROUND]             a plain coloured backdrop, or an ORDERED real place. -> PARTS/ground
[LIGHT]              controlled and even. Nothing falls into murk.
[GRADE]              a clean natural palette, skin held true.
```

**REGISTER changed at 0.4, from `documentary` to `portrait`.** Set 1 asked for a documentary
photograph and got documentary values — grain, grime, available light, a caught expression —
and those are `lede-inuse`'s values. A portrait is composed and its subject knows they are
being photographed, which is this type's whole argument.

## PARTS

**`expertise`** — **the subject must READ as the trade, and DRESS is how.** This is the
clause 0.3 and 0.4 were missing, and 0.4 actively suppressed.

Set 2 ran the same picture six times and dress is what separated the working frames from the
failing ones: a **cycling jersey** made a man holding a helmet a cyclist; a **plain black
shirt** made a man holding a clipper an executive; a **t-shirt and a generic apron** made a
woman holding a Japanese knife somebody who had wandered into a professional kitchen —
which is the owner's own verdict, *"1 phụ nữ bình thường, ăn mặc bình thường không phải
chef"*. **Paint-spattered overalls** made a decorator legible in the messiest frame of the
six.

**Working dress is NOT a credential, and 0.4 banned it by mistake.** That version carried
*"no chef's whites worn as a uniform of rank"* and *"no hi-vis worn as a badge of office"*,
reasoning from G16's `named expert` row. That row governs a printed CLAIM — a name, a title,
an accreditation, a certificate. **Chef's whites are what the job looks like, not an
assertion that anyone is qualified.** Suppressing them removed the only expert signal the
frame had in three of six cells. The ban is now scoped to what is printed and worn as proof:
a lanyard, an ID card, a certificate, a rosette, a sash.

**`address`** — one form, and one narrow exception.

| form | what it is | observations | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `to-lens` | the subject looks into the lens and presents or speaks | **10** | **the form** |
| `interview` | a second person in frame; the subject answers them | 3 | **REFUSED** — owner instruction, 2026-09-09 |

**`interview` is out, and the reason is worse than the owner's word for it.** The
instruction was *"author như đang on mid conversation với ai đó, kiểu này loại ngay lập
tức"*. Set 2 shows why it deserves that: **two people plus a product reads as a SALE.** Cell
4 came back as a tile-shop salesman explaining a cutter to a customer — competent, well lit,
and arguing the exact opposite of the editorial independence this type exists to carry. Cell
3 was worse still, because the binoculars had no role at all: a woman holding them at chest
height in a studio, not using them, talking to somebody off-axis.

**The one exception is the owner's own:** a product whose subject IS communication — a
headset, a conference speaker, an interpreter earbud — where two people talking is the
product working rather than a sales floor.

**`pose`** — **added at 0.10 on owner instruction, and it corrects an over-broad clause of
mine.** Owner, 2026-09-09: *"author cần có đa dạng pose, biểu cảm (để mở cho model tự
chạy)"*.

0.4 added *"the expression is settled — not startled, not mid-flinch, not caught off guard"*
after four of five set-1 frames came back startled. It fixed that and then kept working: **across
twelve renders every subject is centred, square to the lens, hands near chest height, face
neutral.** The clause was doing two jobs and only one of them was wanted.

**The distinction is DELIBERATE against CAUGHT, not neutral against expressive.** A laugh, a
raised brow, a sceptical look, a frown of concentration, a half-turn, a lean on the bench, a
crouch to the product — every one of those is chosen by the subject and belongs here. What
set 1 produced was involuntary: a face reacting to the shutter.

**So pose and expression are INFORMATIVE, not prescribed.** A prompt lists the range and the
model picks; `CONSTRAINTS` keeps only the refusal. This follows the owner's standing
instruction that where a prompt can be opened it should inform rather than dictate — and it
is the round-3 finding applied, since pose is taste and taste is the half that never held
when it was dictated.

**`condition`** — **the product is in as-new condition, always, and this is the clause set 1
was missing.** It is the thing the page is recommending; a reader cannot be asked to want a
soiled one. Set 1 asked for a runner *"at the end of a run"* and got a vest caked in dust,
and for a skillet *"hot and just used"* and got a grimy pan on a dirty hob. **Both were the
owner's first named fault** (2026-09-09: *"sản phẩm bị bẩn"*). Signs of a life lived belong
on the PERSON and on the room, never on the unit.

**`ground`** — two forms, and set 1 reversed which one leads.

| form | texture | value | saturation | observations | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `studio` — a plain backdrop, **light AND coloured, both named** | 0.3 – 2.0 | **0.90** | **0.50** | **12 rendered** | value solved at 0.9; chroma still undershooting |
| `contextual` — the product's own domain | 2.5 – 10.0 | 0.16 – 0.69 | 0.07 – 0.29 | **6** | works only if ORDERED |

**The studio ground has been wrong in nine of nine renders, and the cause is one phrase of
mine.** Owner instruction, 2026-09-09: *"các nền studio cần sáng hơn, nổi bật, bắt mắt hơn"*.
Measured with the ring metric across every studio frame this type has produced:

| | value | saturation |
|---|---|---|
| **our nine, median** | **0.35** | **0.99** |
| the owner's own named studio frame | 0.67 | 0.73 |
| corpus `lede-winner` designed ground | **0.99** | 0.55 |
| corpus `lede-collage` designed ground | **0.90** | 0.49 |

**Every one of the nine prompts said *"a plain studio backdrop in one strong saturated
colour"*, and that phrase reliably returns a deep, dark, fully saturated field** — seven of
the nine came back at saturation 0.99, which is the maximum. It is the same overshoot already
recorded twice elsewhere in the namespace (round 2's cobalt at 0.92, round 3's lineup at
1.00); at nine of nine it is settled rather than suspected.

**The correction was already written in this namespace and this type never imported it.**
ADR-073's designed-ground clause says *"Light AND strongly coloured — both together, since a
dark saturated field and a light quiet field are each only half of what the corpus does."*

### Set 6 proved the cause with a control, and then overshot the other way

Three cells took new wording — *"a LIGHT, clear, vivid colour"* — and one control kept the
old phrase. Measured:

| | value | saturation |
|---|---|---|
| the nine earlier frames, median | 0.35 | 0.99 |
| set 6 control, old wording kept | **0.31** | 0.75 |
| set 6 cell 1, new wording | **0.92** | 0.40 |
| set 6 cell 3, new wording | **0.89** | 0.20 |
| set 6 cell 4, new wording | **0.95** | 0.26 |
| target | 0.90 | 0.50 |

**The phrase was the cause, and one batch settled it**: the three corrected cells landed on
the value target while the control sat at 0.31 with the earlier nine. **But chroma swung to
the opposite extreme** — 0.20 to 0.40 against a target of 0.50, and cell 3 came back
effectively washed out.

**The generalisable lesson, and it is the second time this exact trap has been sprung.** When
a clause names two properties that trade against each other, naming only one lets the model
pay for it with the other. *"One strong saturated colour"* named chroma and paid with value:
0.35 / 0.99. *"A light, clear, vivid colour"* named value and paid with chroma: 0.92 / 0.29.
**Both halves must be stated, as a pair, with a number for each.** ADR-073 said *"both
together"*; this file quoted that sentence at 0.8 and still implemented half of it.

**And the separation test at 0.8 was wrong for pale products.** It read *"the backdrop is
lighter than the subject's clothing and lighter than the product"* — with white baby monitors
that forces the ground to near-white, which is what produced cell 3. The right rule is
`lede-lineup`'s, which this type could also have imported: **the ground separates by VALUE or
by CHROMA — near-white behind dark or strongly coloured units, strongly coloured behind pale
or metallic ones.** A choice the prompt names, not a band it aims at.

**`studio` leads.** It is the owner's own named form, and set 1's cell 3 reproduced it
almost exactly — texture 1.2 against 2.0, saturation 0.75 against 0.73 — and was the only
frame of five that read as an authority. Stripping the place did not weaken the standing; it
concentrated it on the person and the product.

**`contextual` is not dead, it is conditional.** What failed in set 1 was not the location
but its STATE: a cluttered bench, dirty dishes stacked behind the range, a stained apron. A
contextual frame has to be the tidy, bright, working version of that place — the one a
photographer would be shown, not the one nobody cleaned. **Untested in that form**, and it
is what set 2 exists to try.

**So the mechanism is CONTROL, not place.** A reader grants standing to someone who has
their material in order. That is why a plain backdrop works at all, and it is why the same
bench works or fails depending on nothing but its state.

**The ground and the ACT are locked together, and set 2 is where that was learned.** A
product needs a reason to be in the subject's hands, and the reason differs by ground:

| ground | the only coherent act | what happens otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| `studio` | **PRESENTING it** — held up, turned so it reads, offered to the lens | set 2 cell 3: binoculars held at chest height in a studio while talking to somebody. The object had no role and the frame meant nothing |
| `contextual` | **USING it** — the real task, in the place that task belongs | a person posed with a tool in a workshop they are not working in is a catalogue shot with a person in it |

**Never studio-and-talking. Never contextual-and-posing.** Both were rendered in set 2 and
both failed.

## COHERENCE TEST — three questions, answered in writing before any prompt ships

Owner instruction, 2026-09-09: *"check trước khi giao"*. Set 2's failures were not craft
failures — every frame was competently made — they were **logic** failures, and a logic
failure is invisible to a validator and cheap to catch by hand.

1. **Is the product in a correct usage context?** Would this object plausibly be in this
   person's hands, here, right now? If the answer needs a story, the frame does not have one.
2. **Is the presenter actually an author or an expert?** Not asserted — VISIBLE. What in the
   frame, other than a caption nobody may print, tells a reader this person knows? If the
   answer is only *they are holding it*, the frame is a catalogue shot.
3. **What is the logic of the background?** What is that place doing in the argument? A
   studio isolates the product; a real place says the person works there. A background that
   does neither is set dressing, and set 2 shipped three of them.

**A prompt that cannot answer all three in one line each does not ship.** The answers go in
the set file beside the cell, so a later reader can grade the reasoning and not only the
picture.

## NEGATIVE
```
[G6] + a name, byline, caption, title card, lower third, accreditation, lanyard,
badge of office, medal, certificate, diploma, framed award, clipboard,
white coat worn as a credential, a rating, a star row, a review count,
a recognisable real public figure, a minor, any word or number in the frame,
a soiled or damaged product, a cluttered surface, unwashed dishes,
a startled or caught-off-guard expression, a stock-photo grin, a second person,
clothing that gives no clue what the subject does
```

**Set 1's lesson, and it is about REGISTER rather than tidiness for its own sake.** A frame
that shows mess argues *this is real life*, which is `lede-inuse`'s argument. This type
argues *this person knows*, and knowing looks like order. Set 1 asked for a subject *"caught
in the middle of saying something"* and got startled faces in four of five frames —
**composed and mid-sentence are not the same instruction**, and only the first belongs here.

**Two corrections to 0.4's own NEGATIVE, both from set 2.** *"A stained or dirty garment"* is
struck: the **PRODUCT** is what must be spotless, and a tradesperson's working dress may
carry the honest marks of the trade — paint-spattered overalls were the clearest expert
signal in set 2. And *"a second person"* is added, because the owner refused that frame
outright; the exception for communication products is in `PARTS/address`.

**The G14 firewall is the load-bearing clause and it is about REGISTER, not about honesty
of intent.** G14 refuses a generated image that poses as a customer's own. So this frame is
made by a photographer and must look it: **no selfie framing, no arm's-length camera, no
phone held toward a mirror, no front-camera distortion, no vertical hand-held look.** One
of the three frames the owner named on 2026-09-09 is a bathroom shot in exactly that
register, and it is the one form of this type the library may not generate.

**No credential may be printed, worn or displayed.** The subject's standing comes from the
place and the act. The moment a frame carries a title, a certificate or a coat worn to mean
*expert*, it is asserting a qualification nobody granted — which is what G16 refuses when a
text layer states it, and what this type refuses in the picture itself.

**A white coat is not banned as clothing, it is banned as a claim.** `lede-testing` permits
one because a technician at a load bank is doing a job; here there is no apparatus, so a
coat can only be costume.

## WHY THIS IS NO LONGER RESERVED

0.1 and 0.2 shipped `reserved` with `blocked_by: out-of-library-scope`, refused twice over.
Read against the actual text of both refusals, neither reaches this type — and the third
consideration is real and survives as a NEGATIVE clause rather than as a status.

1. **G16 governs THE TEXT LAYER.** Its `named expert` row sits in a rule whose whole scope
   is the words a type is permitted to bake in, alongside prices, ratings and second
   languages. **This type declares no `text_layer`**, so there is nothing for that row to
   waive or refuse. What it was protecting against — a printed claim of expertise — is
   refused outright in NEGATIVE above.
2. **`mapping/slot-rules.md`'s `author` row governs the `author` SLOT.** Its own reasoning
   names the mechanism: *"generating a face to sit under a real byline is a disclosure
   decision rather than an image one"*. The refusal is the BYLINE. A top-N lede is scraped
   as `og:image`, sits beside the page's own headline, and names nobody.
3. **G14 is the one that genuinely binds, and it is not a status block.** It refuses the
   UGC register, not a person. It is now the firewall in NEGATIVE, stated as the visible
   thing it governs: how the frame is shot.

**The fork 0.1 wrote is answered by its own third case.** That section said there were only
two — a real author's photograph, out of scope; or a generated expert, refused — and that
*"there is no third case"*. There is: **an unnamed person, in the product's own domain,
making no claim about their qualifications.** Round 2 found the credential is what carried
the authority and concluded that removing it leaves nothing; round 3 found that the PLACE
and an INTERLOCUTOR can carry it instead. That is 1 observation against 1, which is why
this file is active with a six-case set behind it rather than promoted on argument.

**What would put it back.** If the round-4 set shows readers cannot separate these frames
from `lede-inuse`, the type folds into that one under an `address` axis and this file goes.
That merge condition is written here on purpose, in the same shape `lede-lineup` carries
its own.

## FOUNDING RENDER ROUND

**Round 2, prompt 7, at v0.1 — `partial`.** A man crouched beside a sofa in a half-emptied
room, holding a furniture lifter, addressing the lens. Nothing refused appeared: no name,
byline, title card, lab coat, clipboard, lanyard, seal, star row, logo, word or number. The
finding was that what remains is a **demonstrator**, not an authority — the credential was
carrying the argument, and the distinction from `lede-inuse` reduced to gaze direction. One
failure: the lifter was held at floor level against a prompt asking for chest height.

**Round 3, prompt 7, at v0.2 — `partial`, and it partly reverses the above.** A woman in
running kit on an athletics track, knee sleeve on, answering someone. **A second person
stood at the frame edge with their back to the lens — neither asked for nor forbidden — and
that turned the frame into an INTERVIEW**, a register `lede-inuse` cannot reach. Every
identity constraint held. One failure: the shoes carried a legible footwear brand logo
against a constraint banning any logo, which is why NEGATIVE above now bans the credential
rather than every mark.

**Ground, both rounds, closest match to the photographed clause in the namespace:** round 2
texture 6.8 / value 0.64 / spread 0.88; round 3 texture 6.0 / value 0.65 / spread 0.76,
against the clause's 7.0 / 0.65 / 0.67.

**Three findings, and the first one is mine.**

## PARTS — `composition`, added at 0.7

**Five is the count.** Owner, 2026-09-09: *"top list luôn viết về 5 sản phẩm"*. Every
multi-product form below is built for five, not for "several". (This is a namespace fact
rather than a fact about this type, and it belongs in `registry/toplist-instruction.md` when
that file is next opened.)

| form | what it is | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `single` | one product, the author holding or using it | **10 renders, 8 clean** |
| `field` | the products ranged across one plane, the author beside or behind them | **4 renders, 4 clean** |
| ~~`grid`~~ | one cell per product, the author in one cell | **REMOVED at 0.8** — owner instruction, 5 renders |

**`grid` is the owner's own design and it solves the fault that produced it.** Set 4's panel
cell gave each of three panels a full-length person and put the boot in a band at the bottom
of each; the owner's verdict was *"không nhìn rõ sản phẩm"*. The fix they gave is structural:
*"5 frames giày và 1 frame author giơ like hoặc tương tác gì đó"* — **five cells for five
products, and the author gets a cell of his own rather than the whole frame.**

**The law that follows: a cell is framed to its PRODUCT, not to its person.** A cell exists
to show one product at the largest size it can hold. Where the product needs a body to make
sense — a boot, a harness, a garment — the cell crops to the part of the body that carries
it, and no further. A full-length figure in a cell whose subject is a boot spends the cell on
trousers.

**The author does not have to fill the frame, and may not have to be in it.** The owner:
*"có thể không cần nhìn thấy author nhưng cần sự so sánh"*. In `grid` the author is one cell
— a portrait doing something human, a thumbs-up, an interaction — which keeps a person behind
the comparison while every product gets its own panel. **Whether the author cell is needed at
all is the sharpest open question this type has**, because a five-cell grid with no person in
it is `lede-collage`, not `lede-authority`. Set 5 cell 6 is built to answer it.

**One qualification to `PARTS/expertise`, from set 4.** Cell 5's keyboard reviewer wears a
plain grey top — no trade dress at all — and the frame still works, because **there is no
uniform for a keyboard reviewer**. Where a category has no working dress, the FIELD supplies
the standing: having all five in front of you IS the credential. Dress carries the trades;
the field carries the categories that have none.

## OPEN — does the author compare SEVERAL products?

**Owner question, 2026-09-09**, with four market frames named: *"type này author có so sánh
nhiều sản phẩm không?"*

**The honest answer is yes, and it is probably the shape this type most needs.** A top-N page
is about N products; a lede showing its author holding ONE of them argues less than the page
claims. The named frames carry two distinct shapes this file does not have:

- **author + FIELD** — the reviewer at one side and the whole tested field ranged across the
  frame. The author's presence says *I tested these*, and the field says *these are the N*.
- **one subject, SEVERAL PANELS** — the same person repeated across three cut-out panels on
  one designed ground, each panel a different product worn.

**Neither is declared here and neither is tested.** `products_in_frame: one` stays until a
set renders, because a frontmatter value is a routing promise and this type has no evidence
for that one yet. **The cost of changing it is real and is stated now:**
`mapping/toplist-rules.md` layer 1 refuses `products_in_frame: many` on any page whose input
carries fewer than three products with a photo each — and the input schema has no
`products[]`. Declaring `many` today would make this type unroutable on every page, the same
condition `lede-winner` sits in behind the missing rank field.

One of the named frames also carries baked display type and graphic arrows. **This type
declares no `text_layer` and set 4 does not add one** — that is a separate decision, and
ADR-071 settled which types carry one on evidence rather than on permission.
