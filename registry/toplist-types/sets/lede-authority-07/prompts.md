# `lede-authority` set 7 — pose opened up, and the ground clause finally names both halves

Written against **`lede-authority` v0.10**. Owner instruction, 2026-09-09: *"author cần có đa
dạng pose, biểu cảm (để mở cho model tự chạy)"*.

## Two corrections carry into every cell, and both are corrections to clauses of mine

**1 · The pose clause was doing two jobs and only one was wanted.**

0.4 added *"the expression is settled — not startled, not mid-flinch, not caught off guard"*
after four of five set-1 frames came back startled. It fixed that, and then it kept working:
**across twelve renders every subject is centred, square to the lens, hands near chest
height, face neutral.** Twelve competent frames of the same photograph.

The distinction is **DELIBERATE against CAUGHT**, not neutral against expressive. A laugh, a
raised brow, a sceptical look, concentration, a half-turn, a lean, a crouch to the product —
all chosen by the subject, all fine. What set 1 produced was involuntary: a face reacting to
the shutter. So `POSE` moves into `ELEMENTS` as a **range the model picks from**, and
`CONSTRAINTS` keeps only the refusal.

**2 · The ground clause has now failed twice by naming one property of a pair.**

| wording | names | pays with | measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| *"one strong saturated colour"* ×9 | chroma | **value** | 0.35 / 0.99 |
| *"a light, clear, vivid colour"* ×3 | value | **chroma** | 0.92 / 0.29 |
| target | — | — | **0.90 / 0.50** |

Set 6 proved the cause with a control — three cells changed the phrase and went to 0.89–0.95
while the control kept it and stayed at 0.31 — and then overshot the other way. **A clause
naming two properties that trade against each other must name both, and rule out both failure
modes by name.** Every studio cell below does that, with two colour anchors that are
themselves light AND saturated.

**And the separation test from 0.8 is struck.** It read *"the backdrop is lighter than the
product"*; with white units that forces a near-white ground, which is exactly how set 6 cell 3
washed out. `lede-lineup`'s bimodal rule replaces it, and cells 1 and 2 test its two branches
against each other.

**Cell 6 is the control and it keeps 0.4's flat pose wording**, so the batch shows whether
opening pose actually changes anything rather than assuming it. If cells 1–5 come back varied
and cell 6 comes back centred and neutral like the previous twelve, the clause was the cause.

**Cells 1 and 2 are a matched pair on the separation rule.** Dark units on a light ground
against pale units on a deep ground — if both separate, the bimodal rule holds here as it does
in `lede-lineup`; if the pale-unit cell washes out again, the rule needs something else.

Six products, none used in sets 1–6 or rounds 1–3. Attachments: 5, 5, 1, 1, 5, 1. Cells 1, 2
and 5 render elsewhere (ADR-076). No text layer.

## 1 — five bluetooth speakers · DARK units, light saturated ground, pose OPEN

```
TYPE: lede-authority v0.10 — SET 7 CELL 1
REGISTER: editorial portrait photograph, one frame, no words in it.

[PRODUCT REFERENCES] five attached photos, one per speaker, in the order given. Each is the
            exact reference for that unit — preserve shape, proportions, material, finish,
            colour and every printed mark exactly. Five different makers.

ELEMENTS — assemble and style these as the picture needs.
[CONDITION] every speaker is clean and as-new — grilles unmarked, housings unscuffed.
[FIELD]     four of the five in a row across the lower third on a plane at chest height,
            evenly spaced, each turned so its grille and controls read.
[HERO]      the fifth in the subject's hands.
[SUBJECT]   a live-sound engineer in her thirties, in-ear monitors looped round her neck.
[POSE]      choose one and commit to it — she might laugh, raise a brow at the unit in her
            hands, turn a shoulder to the lens, lean an elbow on the plane, hold the speaker
            up to one ear, or look flatly amused. Anything DELIBERATE. Vary the crop to suit
            the pose you pick; she does not have to be centred or square to the lens.
[ADDRESS]   her attention reaches the lens at some point in the gesture.
[GROUND]    a plain studio backdrop in a colour that is BOTH light AND strongly coloured at
            once — a vivid lemon, a bright coral, a clear sky blue at full strength. NOT a
            pastel, tint or washed tone; NOT a deep, dark or muted one.
[LIGHT]     a soft broad key for the whole group, the backdrop lit evenly and brightly.
[GRADE]     a clean natural palette, skin held true, no cast from the backdrop.

CONSTRAINTS — binding.
· The backdrop is light enough that the dark speakers read against it at a glance.
· Editorial register: made by somebody else. No selfie framing, no arm's-length camera,
  no phone in shot, no front-camera distortion.
· One person only. No second person anywhere in frame.
· Nothing startled, mid-flinch or caught off guard, and no stock-photo grin.
· The four in the row are equal in size, height and lighting.
· No name, caption, title card, lanyard or certificate, and no word or number that names
  or qualifies a person.
· Every unit stays clear of every frame edge.
```
