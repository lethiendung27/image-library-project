# `lede-inuse` set 7 — a room with a life in it, and a body that never stops

Written against **`lede-inuse` v0.11**. Owner, 2026-09-10, on set 6: *"mọi thứ đã ổn trừ scene chưa
lively, chưa sống động"*.

## What set 6 settled

**Everything the owner had asked for before this holds.** Casting 12 of 12 on its first outing.
Light 23 of 23 across sets 4–6. `[DRESS]` in all twelve. The three cases that had never been drawn
all work — the package route returned a scene rather than the packshot this file predicted, the
designed mat is 3 of 3, and `gaze: reflect` landed with the G14 firewall intact at the closest this
type has come to the line.

**The gaze diagnosis holds against its control, and the control is why we know.** With the
turn-back option in the pose range, the two identical draws split — one off-frame, one straight into
the lens. Without it, **0 of 10**. Either draw alone would have settled it wrongly.

## The one thing left, and I wrote both halves of it

**A still room.** `PARTS/environment` met NEGLECT at 0.8 and answered it with CLEAR. Every prompt in
sets 4, 5 and 6 asked for *a kept X — clear worktop*, *clear floor*, *everything in order*. It cured
the dank stairwell and then kept working: set 6 returned a bare bed, an empty dresser, a blank
rendered wall and an unpeopled show kitchen. **The only two frames that read alive are the two whose
rooms are full because of what the room IS** — a rack of chisels, a pegboard of spanners.

**A stopped body.** Cell 7 leans on the wall smiling with the hammer up and the nail not going in.
Cell 11 holds the coat out with the shaver against her chest. **Both are people paused for a
photograph**, which is a portrait even with the eyes away from the lens — the third time the pose
opening has pulled that way.

**Cared-for is not empty, and at ease is not stopped.** That is the whole of this set.

## `[POSE]` and `[COMFORT]` are merged into `[EASE]`

**The removal named at set 5 and deferred twice is taken here**, because set 6 shipped 100 characters
under Rule 6's ceiling and this set adds a lived-in room to every cell. Both clauses landed as
separate slots — pose gave the wall lean and the greenhouse lean, comfort's floor held 12 of 12 — so
what is merged is two working things, not a working one and a dead one.

**Four prompts of the first draft went over Rule 6's ceiling and the gate fired again**, the third
set running. The act anchor turned out to be stated twice — in `[EASE]` and again as a binding
constraint — so set 4's vindicated removal applies to it, and the constraint now carries one wording
for all six cells instead of naming its own verb each time.

**It is a CONSTANT across all six cells, not a variable between them**, so it cannot confound the
comparison this set is making. It could still cost something against sets 5 and 6, and that is
graded below at question 7. If ease weakens across the board, the merge comes back apart.

## RESULT — rendered 2026-09-10, all six opened and graded under ADR-011

Full finding in `lede-inuse.md` at 0.12. **4 pass, 1 partial, 1 fail.**

| # | product | medV | dark | sat | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | pasta machine | 0.62 | 13% | 0.34 | **pass** — herbs, lemons, a jar of spoons, flour on the worktop; dough coming through mid-crank |
| 2 | shoe horn | 0.77 | 4% | 0.24 | **partial** — the named objects arrived and were hung in an evenly spaced row: **staged** |
| 3 | pet hair roller | 0.64 | 4% | 0.20 | **fail** — the owner's own call. A square-cornered rectangle cut into an inch-deep pelt |
| 4 | hose reel | 0.67 | 3% | 0.28 | **pass** — the best frame in the set; every object left rather than placed |
| 5 | CONTROL draw 1 | 0.67 | 5% | 0.35 | **pass** on evidence — furnished but impersonal room, and a grimacing expression |
| 6 | CONTROL draw 2 | 0.78 | 10% | 0.30 | **pass** on evidence — bare worktop, one bowl, nothing personal |

**Prediction by prediction:**

- **Cells 1–4 predicted to carry at least three objects belonging to somebody's life.** 4 of 4.
- **Predicted none reads staged.** **Wrong on cell 2** — coats, scarf and basket in a neat row. The
  fix is at 0.12: name the objects AND name that they were put down mid-life.
- **Control predicted to come back with empty surfaces.** **Half right, and the half that was wrong
  is the more useful half.** Draw 2 was bare; draw 1 was furnished — shelves, crockery, a knife
  block, a kettle — and still had nothing in it belonging to anyone. **The axis is PERSONAL against
  IMPERSONAL, not full against empty**, and fixtures do not count. 0 of 2 control draws personal.
- **Control predicted to show a stopped body in at least one draw.** **Wrong, 0 of 2** — both came
  back mid-act. **The act anchor is unproven**: set 6's paused bodies are better explained by their
  standing full-length framing. Kept and flagged.
- **Question 7, did the `[EASE]` merge cost anything?** **No.** Four good expressions from the
  merged longer range; the two control draws on set 6's shorter range gave one grimace and one good.

**The fail is the finding, and the clause caused it.** Cell 3 asked for *a clean stripe* and *a hard
edge where the roller has run*. **A hard boundary is right where the physics makes one** — a melt
front, a wetted edge, a wiped film, all three landed in earlier sets — **and wrong where it does
not.** Fibres lifted off upholstery do not cut square. Written up at 0.12 as a rule about the
material rather than about the wording.
