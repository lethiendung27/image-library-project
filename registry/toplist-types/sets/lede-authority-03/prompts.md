# `lede-authority` set 3 — dress carries the expert, and the act is locked to the ground

Written against **`lede-authority` v0.5**. Owner verdict on set 2, 2026-09-09: two frames
refused outright for the author being *"on mid conversation với ai đó"*; *"một số sản phẩm
giơ lên trong nền studio thì ok"*; the knife cell *"vô lý"* — *"1 phụ nữ bình thường, ăn mặc
bình thường không phải chef"*; the binoculars cell *"vô lý"* for holding a product she is
not using; the barber *"không giống typical barber"*.

## What set 2 got wrong, and the worst of it was a clause I wrote

**v0.4's CONSTRAINTS banned working dress.** Every cell carried some version of *"no chef's
whites worn as a uniform of rank"*, *"no hi-vis worn as a badge of office"*, *"no club or
team kit"*. That came from G16's `named expert` row — but **that row governs a printed
CLAIM**: a name, a title, an accreditation. Chef's whites are not a claim that anybody is
qualified; they are what the job looks like. I removed the only expert signal three of the
six frames had, and then the frames failed for having no expert signal.

The set proves it against itself. **A cycling jersey** made cell 5 work. **A plain black
shirt** made cell 1 an executive holding a clipper. **A t-shirt and a generic apron** made
cell 2 a visitor in somebody else's kitchen. **Paint-spattered overalls** made cell 6
legible as a decorator in the messiest frame of the six.

Three changes carry into every cell below:

- **Dress names the trade.** Working dress is required, not banned. What stays banned is
  proof worn as proof — a lanyard, an ID badge, a certificate, a rosette, a sash.
- **No second person.** `interview` is refused. Set 2 showed why it deserves to be: two
  people plus a product reads as a **sale**, and cell 4 came back as a tile-shop salesman
  explaining a cutter to a customer. The only exception is a product whose subject IS
  communication, and none of the six below is one.
- **The act is locked to the ground.** Studio means **PRESENTING** — held up, turned so it
  reads. A real place means **USING** — the actual task. Cell 3 broke the lock (a studio, a
  product nobody was using, a conversation) and there was nothing left to believe.

## The cells, and what set 3 is actually asking

Set 2 never tested a contextual frame with a subject who reads as the trade — cell 2 had the
tidy place and the wrong person, cell 6 had the right person and a wrecked place. **So four
cells here are `contextual` + `using`, which is the form with the most to prove**, and two
hold the established studio form to isolate dress.

| cell | product | expert & dress | ground | act |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | welding helmet | welder — leathers, gauntlets | contextual, fabrication shop | using |
| 2 | pastry piping set | pastry chef — whites | contextual, patisserie | using |
| 3 | straight razor | barber — barber's smock, own sharp fade | contextual, barbershop | using |
| 4 | climbing harness | mountain guide — climbing kit | **studio** | presenting |
| 5 | surveyor's laser level | surveyor — hi-vis, hard hat | contextual, site | using |
| 6 | cordless impact driver | **none — a plain t-shirt** | **studio** | presenting | **CONTROL** |

**Cell 6 is the control and it isolates DRESS on the form that already works.** It is cell 4's
structure exactly — studio, presenting, clean product, composed subject — with the trade
signal removed. If cell 4 works and cell 6 does not, dress is the variable and
`PARTS/expertise` is right. If both work, the studio form carries standing on its own and
dress only matters in a real place.

Cells 3 and 2 are deliberate re-runs of set 2's two named failures **as arguments, on new
products**: a barber who looks like a barber, and a kitchen professional who looks like one.

## Coherence test, run before shipping

Owner instruction: *"check trước khi giao"*. Three questions per cell, per `lede-authority`
v0.5's COHERENCE TEST section.

| cell | product in a correct usage context? | is the presenter visibly an expert? | what is the background doing? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes — a welding helmet is worn to weld, and he is at the weld | leathers, gauntlets, a scarred bench | the shop says this is his trade, not a hire |
| 2 | yes — piping is the task the set exists for | chef's whites, a piped tray already done | a working patisserie says she does this daily |
| 3 | yes — a razor is used on a face, at a chair | barber's smock, his own sharp haircut | the shop is his; the chair is his station |
| 4 | **presented, not used** — studio, so the act is showing it | climbing kit, chalked hands, rope over shoulder | nothing. The studio removes the place so the product and the person carry it alone |
| 5 | yes — a laser level is set up and read on site | hi-vis, hard hat, site boots | the site says he is working, not demonstrating |
| 6 | presented, studio | **NO — plain t-shirt, no trade signal.** This is the control's whole point | nothing, deliberately |

**Cell 6 fails question 2 on purpose and ships anyway**, because a set in which every cell is
built to pass cannot tell anybody which clause did the work.

Six products, none used in sets 1–2 or rounds 1–3. One attachment each. No text layer.
`ELEMENTS` informative, `CONSTRAINTS` binding; every cell read across both blocks before
shipping.
