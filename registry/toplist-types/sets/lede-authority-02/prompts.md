# `lede-authority` set 2 — control is the constant, address and ground are the variables

Written against **`lede-authority` v0.4**, the version set 1 forced. Owner verdict on set 1,
2026-09-09: cell 6 `fail`, and the rest *"nhìn scene, setup không giống chuyên gia hay
authority mà giống in-use hơn (sản phẩm bị bẩn, cảnh lộn xộn, logic sản phẩm trong ảnh chưa
tốt)"*.

## What set 1 got wrong, and it was the type file rather than the prompts

v0.3 stated the mechanism as *"the place is doing the credentialling"*, so six prompts were
written for lived-in realism — a worn apron, a pan *"hot and just used"*, a runner *"at the
end of a run"*, a bench with *"the ordinary clutter of the trade"*. Every one of those
phrases is mine, and every one of them produced exactly what it asked for.

**Four contextual cells, four readings of `lede-inuse`. One studio cell, one authority.**
The measurements say the same thing: cell 3 reproduced the owner's own named studio form
almost exactly — texture 1.2 against 2.0, saturation 0.75 against 0.73 — while the four
contextual cells scattered from texture 2.5 to 6.7 and none of them read as standing.

So **v0.4 inverts the mechanism to CONTROL**: a composed subject, a product in as-new
condition, and a setting either ordered or absent. Three things follow into every prompt
below and none of them is a variable any more:

- **The product is clean and as-new.** It is what the page is recommending. Set 1's vest
  came back caked in dust and its skillet grimy, both because the prompt asked for it.
- **The setting is ordered.** Not sterile, not styled to death — the tidy working version of
  the place, which is the one a photographer would actually be shown.
- **The subject is composed, not caught.** Set 1 asked for someone *"caught in the middle of
  saying something"* and got startled faces in four of five frames. **Composed and
  mid-sentence are not the same instruction.**

The REGISTER line also changes, from `documentary` to `portrait`. Documentary values — grain,
grime, available light, a caught expression — are `lede-inuse`'s values, and asking for them
is how set 1 ended up there.

## The cells

The type's open question is no longer *what makes standing* — it is **which `address` form
carries it, and whether an ORDERED contextual place works as well as no place at all.**

| cell | product | address | ground | isolates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | professional hair clipper | `to-lens` | studio | **replicates set 1 cell 3 on a new product** — the only form that has worked, at 1 observation |
| 2 | Japanese chef's knife | `to-lens` | ordered contextual | the same address with the place put back, TIDY |
| 3 | birding binoculars | `interview` | studio | `interview` clean and unconfounded — set 1 cell 1 carried it under grime |
| 4 | ceramic tile cutter | `interview` | ordered contextual | both new mechanisms together |
| 5 | road cycling helmet | presenting | studio | the holding-it-up gesture, on a clean product this time |
| 6 | HVLP paint sprayer | `to-lens` | **cluttered contextual — CONTROL** | **isolates GRIME from PLACE** |

**Cell 6 is the control and it is expected to fail.** Set 1 confounded two things: every
failing cell was both contextual AND messy, so *place* and *state* cannot be told apart in
that data. Cell 6 holds the address and the clean product constant and puts back only the
mess. If cell 2 works and cell 6 does not, the variable is STATE and `contextual` survives
as a conditional form. If both fail, `contextual` is dead and this type is a studio type.
**Set 1's cell 6 failed exactly as predicted, which is why a predicted failure is worth a
render.**

Six products, none used in set 1 or in rounds 1–3. One attachment each. No text layer.
`ELEMENTS` informative, `CONSTRAINTS` binding; every cell read across both blocks before
shipping.

```

            hand at chest height, his grip the practised one.
[ADDRESS]   looking into the lens, composed, speaking evenly as though answering a question
            he has answered before.
[GROUND]    a plain studio backdrop in one strong saturated colour, filling the frame
            behind him. No room, no set, no props but the clipper.
[LIGHT]     a soft key from the front with the backdrop lit evenly, and clean highlights
            along the clipper's body.
[GRADE]     a clean natural palette, skin held true, no cast from the backdrop.

CONSTRAINTS — binding.
· Editorial register: this photograph is made by somebody else. No selfie framing, no
  arm's-length camera, no phone in shot, no front-camera distortion.
· Nothing credentials him: no name, caption, title card, certificate, framed award,
  lanyard, medal or clipboard.
· No printed word or number anywhere that names or qualifies a person.
· His expression is settled. Not startled, not mid-flinch, not caught off guard.
· One person only, adult, not a recognisable public figure.
· The clipper stays clear of every frame edge.
```

## Grading

| # | question | what it changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cell 1 — does the studio form work a second time, on a new product?** | it is the type's only working form and it stands on one observation. Two makes it the SKELETON's default |
| 2 | **Cell 2 beside cell 6 — does an ORDERED place work where a cluttered one does not?** | whether `contextual` survives as a conditional form or the type becomes studio-only. This is the set's main question |
| 3 | **Cell 3 — does `interview` carry standing when it is clean and the interlocutor is small?** | set 1's cell 1 had the form under grime and a bystander filling a third of the frame. `interview` is still at one clean observation, from round 3 |
| 4 | **Cell 4 — do both new mechanisms together beat either alone?** | whether the SKELETON should lead with one form or offer two |

| 8 | **How many CONSTRAINTS held across the six?** | round 3 ran 14 of 18; set 1 is not yet counted. The running record for the two-block form |
