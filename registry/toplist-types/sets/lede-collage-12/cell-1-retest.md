# `lede-collage` set 12 cell 1 — retest, three gradient schemes against one contrast rule

Written against **`lede-collage` v0.22**. Owner, 2026-09-10: *"màu nền có thể không phải opposite
trên color wheel mà còn có thể analogous. đảm bảo tương phản giữa nền, sản phẩm và plate."*

## What went wrong, and it was my clause

0.19 wrote *"two hues OPPOSITE each other on the colour wheel"* to fix set 8's gradient, which
travelled only 65° against a corpus of 174–177°. It fixed the travel and bought a worse fault:
**opposite hues meet in a muddy midpoint**, and set 12 cell 1 stood a dark green hero chair
exactly on the orange-to-cyan transition, where it barely reads.

**The corpus never said opposite.** `PARTS/ground` records hue spreads of 1°, 2°, 27°, 47°, 98°,
174° and 177° — a continuum. Half a wheel is one option, not the rule.

## The three, and what separates them

**Travel is the variable; the contrast rule is identical in all three.** Three different
categories, none used in any earlier set, because a rule that holds only on a product it has
already seen is not a rule.

| # | gradient | products |
|---|---|---|
| A | **ANALOGOUS** — two hues about a sixth of the wheel apart | 5 garden benches |
| B | **OPPOSITE** — half the wheel, as set 12 ran it, now with the contrast rule | 5 hammocks |
| C | **ONE FLAT TONE** — no gradient at all, the corpus's other observed form | 5 paddle boards |

The contrast rule is stated as things rather than as a measurement, which is the only kind of
clause this type obeys: **the hero carries a colour the ground does not**, and **the border is
darker than every part of the ground**.

> **What each answers.** If A and C read cleanly and B does not, travel is the fault and the
> clause should name a hue family. If all three read cleanly, the contrast rule is doing the
> work and travel is free — which is what `PARTS/ground`'s own corpus spread implies.
